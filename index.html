Null_nx Expansion Matrix

A C60 cage, the Millennium problems as small real computations, and a benchmark, all computed live in your browser.


    
Drag the cage to turn it. Geometry is an ideal truncated icosahedron with equal bonds; real C60 has two bond lengths averaging about 1.42 Å.

  






  
  

    
    

    
Plots Z(t), the real-valued form of ζ(½ + it). Each crossing of zero is a nontrivial zero on the critical line. Computed with Borwein's algorithm; the check against published zeros is real.

  






  
  

    
    
    
    

    

  






  
  

    
    
    

    
Hexagons join side to side, ring by ring. Each step lengthens the side by your portion of √2. Brighter hexagons mark the six arms. A flat patch cannot close into a sphere; closed fullerene cages need exactly 12 pentagons.

  






  
  

    
    
    
    
Press Run to climb.

    
Each step turns a length L into L + k·√2·L, kept exactly as a pair of whole numbers (x, y) meaning x + y√2. Then x/y is checked against √2 computed a different way. It runs in a background worker, so you can cancel it.

  






  
  

    
    

    
Chinese remainder theorem (Sunzi Suanjing, c. 3rd–5th century). Since 252 = 4 × 9 × 7 and 4, 9, 7 share no factors, the three remainders identify every number from 0 to 251 uniquely. The dials only line up again at 252.

  






  
  

    
    

    
Hückel model: one π orbital per carbon, nearest-neighbour hopping, eigenvalues in units of |β|. The 90 bonds split into 30 shorter 6:6 bonds and 60 longer 5:6 bonds; the slider strengthens the short ones. Left: energy levels (cyan = filled with the 60 π electrons). Right: the gap as the ratio grows.
